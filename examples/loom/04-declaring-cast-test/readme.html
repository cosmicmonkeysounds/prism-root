Lesson 04 — declaring your cast

In a film script the casting director picks who plays each role. In Loom you tell the runtime up front: here are the speakers in this document, and this is what they're called. The cast declaration does that job.

The minimum

cast WREN

That alone is enough — it announces that WREN is a speaker. If anyone later writes WREN at the left margin and indents a line underneath, Loom understands it as Wren's dialogue.

But you'll usually want to attach a few details. Indent them under the cast line, each prefixed with a dot: 

cast WREN
  .label "Wren the Fisher"
  .voice female_alto

The two most common dots:

  • .label — the human-readable name shown to the audience. "WREN" is the script-internal id; "Wren the Fisher" is what the player actually sees on screen.
  • .voice — a hint for the audio layer. The id of a TTS profile or a recorded performer.

You can add any number of other dotted properties; the runtime preserves them for the host to read.

Why declare at all?

Loom's validator checks that every SPEAKER you mention has a matching cast declaration. Forgetting one isn't catastrophic — the story still plays — but you get a warning. The declaration is your one place to:

  • Spell-check your speakers before opening night.
  • Hand the audio team a complete voice list.
  • Wire each character to a portrait, a voice profile, or a performer.

What you just learned

  • cast IDENT introduces a speaker.
  • Indented dotted properties — .label, .voice, and friends — attach metadata.
  • Every speaker should be declared, or the validator will complain.
Where to put declarations. Cast lines usually go just under the document header, before any sections. They can also live in a separate "characters" file that other stories import — but that's a Phase 3 topic.
Try this. Add a .voice property to MAREN in lesson.loom. The runtime will pick it up automatically; the player won't see anything different until the audio layer reads it, but the data is now part of the compiled bundle.
