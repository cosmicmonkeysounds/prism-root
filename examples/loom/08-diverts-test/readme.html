Lesson 08 — diverts

A divert is a one-way jump. The playhead leaves the current position and starts playing the named section instead. Diverts are how you break a story out of strict top-to-bottom order.

The arrow

-> section_name

That's it. When the playhead reaches that line, it jumps to section_name and starts playing it.

From inside a choice

The most common pattern: a choice fires a divert.

WREN
  Where to?

  * Up the lighthouse.   -> lighthouse
  * Down to the docks.   -> docks

Reading top-to-bottom you can almost diagram the flow with your eye. That's the point — the arrow looks like an arrow.

Standalone diverts

A divert doesn't have to live under a choice. Use it on its own line at the end of a section to override the playhead's default "fall through":

-- side_chat

WREN
  Quick aside.

-> main_thread

-- main_thread

WREN
  And we're back.

Without the arrow the playhead would fall through to whatever section comes next in source order, which might not be what you want.

A small branching story

Open lesson.loom — it's a complete branching scene. Two branches each end with a final divert into a shared ending section. That pattern (branch out, converge back) is the workhorse of branching writing.

What you just learned

  • -> section_name jumps the playhead.
  • Diverts work inline on a choice or as a standalone line.
  • Use a final divert to override fall-through when a section ends.
Try this. Add a third choice in lesson.loom that diverts to a new secret section. Make the secret section end with -> ending so it converges with the others.
