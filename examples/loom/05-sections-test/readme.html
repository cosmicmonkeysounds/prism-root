Lesson 05 — sections and the playhead

A document longer than a page becomes hard to navigate. Loom breaks documents into sections — named chunks that the runtime plays one at a time. Think of them as scenes you can jump to.

The header

Two hyphens at the start of a line, then a name:

-- start

WREN
  Welcome.

-- next

WREN
  Still here?

Each -- opens a new section. Everything until the next -- (or the end of the file) belongs to that section.

The playhead

When the runtime loads a document, it places a playhead at the first section and starts playing through its lines. When the section runs out of content, the playhead falls through to the next section in the file. Reaching the last section ends the show.

So lesson.loom:

-- one
WREN
  First scene.

-- two
WREN
  Second scene.

-- three
WREN
  Third scene.

plays as three dialogue beats in order, then ends.

Named jumps

Sections only matter once you start jumping between them — that's what -> section diverts do in lesson 8. For now, the takeaway is just that -- is how you mark the boundary.

Anonymous sections

-- with no name still works. The runtime auto-names anonymous sections (__anon_0, __anon_1, …) and you can't jump to them, but they're occasionally useful as inline scene breaks. 

-- start
WREN
  Begin.

--
WREN
  An unnamed beat.

-- end

What you just learned

  • -- name opens a named section.
  • The playhead falls through to the next section automatically when one ends.
  • Anonymous sections (-- with no id) are legal but un-jump-to-able.
Why sections matter. They're the unit Loom remembers in the ledger ("Wren visited investigate twice"), the target of diverts ("-> investigate"), the scope of each visit blocks, and the snapshot the runtime saves when you save a game. Sections are how Loom carves a story into pieces it can think about.
Try this. Add a fourth section -- ending to lesson.loom with one final dialogue line. The runtime will play through all four in order and stop.
