Lesson 07 — choices

This is the first lesson where the audience does something. A choice pauses the playhead and asks "which way?" The lines under each option play only if the player picks that option.

The two markers

Two characters introduce a choice:

  • * — once-only. After it's taken it disappears from the menu the next time the player visits the same choice.
  • + — sticky. Stays available every time. Good for "talk to" hub menus.

The shape

WREN
  What brings you?

  * I'm here to help.
    WREN
      Thank you.
  * I just want to look around.
    WREN
      Suit yourself.

How that runs:

  1. Wren says her line.
  2. The playhead pauses, surfaces two choices to the player.
  3. The player picks one.
  4. The lines indented under that option play.
  5. The playhead resumes after the choice group.

Sticky choices

A sticky choice (+) is exactly the same except it doesn't go away. Hub menus mix the two:

-- hub

WREN
  Where to?

  * Ask about the bell.
    -> bell_question
  * Ask about the keeper.
    -> keeper_question
  + Goodbye.
    -> ending

The two question choices each disappear after being taken once. Goodbye is always there.

Choices with diverts

You'll often want a choice to jump the player to another section rather than play content inline. Stick a -> target on the same line as the choice label: 

* I'll go to the lighthouse.  -> lighthouse
* I'll go to the bell tower.   -> tower

Diverts are the subject of the next lesson.

What you just learned

  • * opens a once-only choice; + opens a sticky one.
  • Lines indented under a choice play only when that choice is taken.
  • A choice can carry an inline -> target divert.
  • Loom collects adjacent choice lines into a single menu the player picks from.
How the runtime remembers. When the player takes a once-only choice, Loom writes a "this choice was spent" mark into the ledger. The next time the playhead reaches the same choice group, that option is filtered out before the menu is built. You'll see this directly when we get to ledger queries in lesson 13.
